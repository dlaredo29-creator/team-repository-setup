Motion
It sees: Motion in the PIR sensor's field of view, or no motion.

It calls you:
- Nobody: The PIR output is inactive.
- Rushing: The PIR output has stayed active for 2 seconds.
- Unhurried: The PIR output has stayed active for 5 seconds.

The PIR module may keep its output active for a few seconds after movement stops. These duration labels do not measure actual walking or running speed.
It does:
- Unhurried: Displays a slow animation on the OLED.
- Rushing: Displays an alert on the OLED.
- Nobody: Turns the OLED off.
After the PIR output goes inactive, the OLED keeps Rushing on screen for 2 seconds or Unhurried for 5 seconds, then turns off. During that hold it shows PIR: 0 and freezes the motion duration.
The OLED shows the PIR's digital reading and how long its output stayed active.
For them: It shows the PIR's motion signal, its active duration, and the last detected state briefly after motion ends.

To them: It labels you based on how long the PIR output stays active, not your actual pace.

It refuses: To claim it knows your speed or distance.

Set aside: Loud beeping sounds and notifications.

Sits next to: Flock cameras or Hansel and Gretel.

The tension: Being tracked and given a label that might not accurately represent who you are or what you are doing.
